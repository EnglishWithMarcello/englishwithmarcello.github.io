I'll tell you about two different religions: hockey in Canada and soccer in Italy.

**<span class="c-aqua">Hockey in Canada: more than just a sport</span>** In Canada, Saturday night is special. "Hockey Night in Canada" airs on CBC at 7 pm. Everyone stops: stores close, streets are empty, families gather in front of the TV. You must cheer for an _<span class="c-red">NHL</span>_ team (I cheered for the Toronto Maple Leafs), know at least 5 famous players, and hate at least one American team.

**<span class="c-aqua">Soccer in Italy: pure passion</span>** Here soccer isn't just a sport: it's life, politics, geography, and family history. One game can unite or divide generations. Every family argues about soccer at least once a week, derbies literally stop cities, and there are stadium chants everyone knows by heart.

My _<span class="c-red">bandwagon</span>_ fan moment: Italy vs. England, Euro 2020 final. I was in a square in Toronto with tons of Italian-Canadians. When Italy won on penalties, I was overjoyed — for one night, I felt Italian too.

**<span class="c-aqua">Absurd comparison:</span>**

- Season: Canada — 6 months on the ice. Italy — 10 months on grass
- Teams: Canada — 20 NHL teams. Italy — 20 Serie A teams + thousands of local teams
- Fights: Canada — allowed. Italy — well, they still happen anyway

What I'm learning is that explaining the offside rule to a Canadian is difficult, and trying to explain _<span class="c-red">body checking</span>_ to an Italian is even harder.

National sports are windows into a culture. Hockey reflects Canada: tough, direct, fast, but fair. Soccer reflects Italy: technical, passionate, strategic, and full of history.
