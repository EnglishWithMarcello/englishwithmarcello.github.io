<p>Hey, there! Today, let's bust some <strong>myths</strong> about Canada that I often hear in Italy.</p>
<p><strong>#1 There are polar bears on the streets</strong><br>Reality: Polar bears live in the Arctic. I'm from Toronto. It's like saying there are penguins in Milan because it's cold in winter. But I admit: I have seen black bears. A few times, at the <strong>cottage</strong>, a mother bear crossed the road with her cubs.</p>
<p><strong>#2 You always have barbecues even in the snow</strong><br>Reality: This is true! My dad has grilled in -20°C. "Snow doesn't stop a good steak," he used to say.</p>
<p><strong>#3 Everyone lives in a cabin in the woods</strong><br>Reality: Toronto has 3 million people. Vancouver is ultra-modern. Montreal is more European than many European cities. We also have skyscrapers, streetcars, and traffic jams.</p>
<p><strong>#4 You all speak French</strong><br>Reality: Only Quebec is truly bilingual. In Toronto, if you try speaking French, people will look at you puzzled, as if you were speaking in code.</p>
<p><strong>#5 You all know all the Native Americans</strong><br>Reality: Canada has many different <strong>First Nations</strong>, with completely different cultures, languages, and traditions. It's like saying all Europeans are the same.</p>
<p><strong>What's really typical of Canada:</strong></p>
<ul><li>Poutine (fries with cheese curds and gravy)</li><li>Maple syrup on everything</li><li>Saying "about" in a funny way (Americans notice right away)</li><li>Being obsessed with the weather</li><li>Saying sorry for everything (even when it's not our fault)</li></ul>
