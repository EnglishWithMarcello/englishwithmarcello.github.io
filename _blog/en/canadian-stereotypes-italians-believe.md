Let's bust some _<span class="c-red">myths</span>_ about Canada that I often hear in Italy.

**<span class="c-aqua">#1 There are polar bears on the streets</span>** Reality: Polar bears live in the Arctic. I'm from Toronto. It's like saying there are penguins in Milan because it's cold in winter. But I admit: I have seen black bears. A few times, at the _<span class="c-red">cottage</span>_, a mother bear crossed the road with her cubs.

**<span class="c-aqua">#2 You always have barbecues even in the snow</span>** Reality: This is true! My dad has grilled in -20°C. "Snow doesn't stop a good steak," he used to say.

**<span class="c-aqua">#3 Everyone lives in a cabin in the woods</span>** Reality: Toronto has 3 million people. Vancouver is ultra-modern. Montreal is more European than many European cities. We also have skyscrapers, streetcars, and traffic jams.

**<span class="c-aqua">#4 You all speak French</span>** Reality: Only Quebec is truly bilingual. In Toronto, if you try speaking French, people will look at you puzzled, as if you were speaking in code.

**<span class="c-aqua">#5 You all know all the Native Americans</span>** Reality: Canada has many different _<span class="c-red">First Nations</span>_, with completely different cultures, languages, and traditions. It's like saying all Europeans are the same.

**<span class="c-aqua">What's really typical of Canada:</span>**

- Poutine (fries with cheese curds and gravy)
- Maple syrup on everything
- Saying "about" in a funny way (Americans notice right away)
- Being obsessed with the weather
- Saying sorry for everything (even when it's not our fault)
